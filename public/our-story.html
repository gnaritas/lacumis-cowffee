Our Story

“La Cumis” is what you
call a good girlfriend

So this café is named after the whole community that showed up — one sweet-potato empanada at a time. The cow in “cowffee”? That’s a nod to a cow-print coffee trailer out in California that Hailey followed online before she ran her own.

See where we are now Try the menu

Middle school

Sweet potatoes into empanadas

Hailey liked to bake. One day her dad came home with a pile of sweet potatoes, and she and her mom — Hortencia, who chefed at Green Acres in Bradley Fair for 13 years — turned them into empanadas.

“And shockingly, there was a lot of people wanting to buy it,” Hailey told the Wichita Eagle. “They were like, ‘When are you going to make more? Do you make other breads?’”

The home bakery

Sold from the kitchen, on Facebook

The family couldn’t eat all the empanadas, so they sold them from home. The demand kept growing — weekend bakes added gorditas, conchas and croissants, and a following grew with them.

2024

The cow-print trailer

The family bought a food trailer, painted it black-and-white cow-print, and parked it on the Northside on weekends — 21st & Fairview. Hailey, a coffee-shop fan, taught herself one Mexican-inspired drink after another until the trailer’s menu was its own thing.

2026

Home: 2040 W 21st St. N

At 21, Hailey is opening her first brick-and-mortar — the old Blend Nutrition space near 21st & Amidon, with dine-in seating. The trailer keeps its weekend post at 21st & Fairview: it takes a short break while the storefront gets going, then heads back out.
